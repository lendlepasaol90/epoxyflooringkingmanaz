Local epoxy flooring

A coating is only as good as the concrete under it.

Kingman sits a little over 3,300 feet up, which gives us a climate that is harder on concrete than most people expect. Garages bake through the summer, then drop near freezing on winter nights. Slabs expand and contract with every swing. Dust works into the pores. Hot tires come off I-40 and sit on the same patch of floor every evening. A thin roll-on paint simply does not hold up to that for long.

Our work starts with the slab itself. We grind the surface with diamond tooling, fill cracks and pitted areas, check for moisture and old sealers, and only then build a coating system matched to the room. Sometimes that means a classic epoxy base with a decorative flake broadcast. Sometimes it is a polyaspartic topcoat for UV resistance at the garage door line, or a flexible polyurea base for a detached shop that sees big temperature swings.

Whatever the system, the goal is the same: a floor that is easier to clean, harder to stain, and still bonded tight years from now. If you want to see what that looks like in practice, start with our garage floor coatings or browse the full list of services below.

Why homeowners and businesses call us

The work you never see is the part we care about most.

Grinding, not etching

Acid etching leaves residue and an uneven profile. Diamond grinding opens the concrete evenly and removes the weak top layer, so the primer bonds to sound material.

Moisture checked before we quote

Older slabs without a vapor barrier can push moisture up through a coating. We test first and, when needed, specify a moisture-tolerant primer rather than hoping for the best.

Products picked for the conditions

A west-facing garage with the door open all afternoon needs a different topcoat than an interior laundry room. We explain the trade-offs in plain language.

Clean, scheduled job sites

We use dust-collecting grinders, protect walls and doors, and give you a realistic timeline for walking, parking and moving things back in.

Garage epoxy flooring

Garages here do more than park cars.

Around Kingman, the garage is often a workshop, a gym, overflow storage and the spot where the side-by-side gets hosed off after a weekend on the trails. Bare concrete takes a beating from all of it. Oil soaks in, tire marks never quite scrub out, and the fine gray dust that comes off an unsealed slab ends up on everything stored nearby.

A full-broadcast flake system is our most requested garage floor for good reason. The flakes hide dust and small debris between cleanings, the texture gives your shoes something to grip, and a clear polyaspartic topcoat keeps the colors from fading where afternoon sun reaches through the open door. For shops with jacks and rolling toolboxes, we can build the system thicker and add more aggregate to the top.

We also handle the details that make a garage feel finished: coating the stem walls a few inches up, cleaning up the expansion joints, and feathering the transition at the driveway edge so it does not catch.

Inside the home

Residential, decorative and metallic floors.

Coatings are not just for the garage. Many of our residential projects are laundry rooms, mudrooms, casitas, converted garages and open living areas where the owner wants a seamless surface that stands up to pets, kids and boots.

Residential epoxy flooring

A coated interior floor has no grout lines to scrub and nothing to trap pet hair. We can keep it simple with a solid color and satin finish, or add subtle flake for texture. Interior work uses low-odor products where possible, and we plan the schedule around how you live in the house.

Metallic epoxy flooring

Beyond standard epoxy

Polyaspartic, polyurea and outdoor coatings.

“Epoxy” has become shorthand for any coated concrete floor, but epoxy is only one ingredient. It bonds and builds thickness beautifully, yet it is not the best choice for every layer. Where sunlight, speed or flexibility matter, other chemistries do the job better.

Polyaspartic coatings cure in hours rather than days and stay clear under UV, which makes them our go-to topcoat for garages and doorways. Polyurea coatings stretch and recover, so they suit slabs that move — think uninsulated metal shops or older garages with a few working cracks.

Outside, a patio and concrete coating has to handle direct sun, pool water and bare feet. We use textured, light-reflective systems that stay comfortable and slip-resistant, and we steer people away from slick, glossy finishes on anything that gets wet.

Commercial & industrial flooring

Floors that keep a business running.

Kingman’s position on the I-40 and US 93 corridor brings a steady mix of trucking, distribution, automotive and light manufacturing work. Those floors deal with forklifts, pallet jacks, hydraulic fluid, and constant foot traffic. A coating has to resist chemicals, hold striping, and come back clean with a mop or scrubber.

For commercial epoxy flooring, we build systems for service bays, restaurant kitchens, retail floors, clinics and offices, often working nights or weekends so you stay open. Warehouse floor coatings focus on abrasion resistance, dust-proofing and clearly marked aisles, safety zones and loading areas.

Larger spaces are usually done in phases. We map the floor with you before we start so racking, equipment and traffic can move around the work.

Installation process

How a coated floor goes in.

Timelines vary with the system and the season, but the sequence rarely changes.

  1. 01

    Walkthrough and testing

    We measure the space, look for cracks, spalling and old coatings, and run moisture checks. You get a written scope and price.

  2. 02

    Diamond grinding

    The slab is ground to an open, even profile with dust collection running the whole time. Edges are handled with smaller tools.

  3. 03

    Repairs

    Cracks are chased and filled, pitted areas patched, and control joints cleaned. Repairs are ground flush so they disappear under the coating.

  4. 04

    Primer and base coat

    A penetrating primer or base coat goes down to lock into the concrete. This layer does most of the bonding work.

  5. 05

    Broadcast or pigment

    Flake is broadcast into the wet base, metallic pigments are worked, or a solid color is built — depending on the system you chose.

  6. 06

    Topcoat and cure

    Excess flake is scraped, the floor is vacuumed, and a clear topcoat seals everything. We leave you with walk, park and cleaning instructions.

Benefits of a professional install

What you actually get for the investment.

A good coating changes how a space works day to day. These are the differences our customers mention most once the floor has been in use for a while.

  • Cleanup takes minutes

    Spills sit on the surface instead of soaking in. Most messes come up with a dust mop, a squeegee or a damp pad.

  • No more concrete dust

    Unsealed slabs shed fine powder that settles on shelves and gets tracked into the house. A sealed floor stops that at the source.

  • Stain and chemical resistance

    Motor oil, brake fluid, fertilizer and pool chemicals are far less likely to leave a permanent mark.

  • A brighter room

    Lighter colors and glossy finishes bounce light around, which helps in garages and shops with only a couple of fixtures.

  • Better traction

    Flake and aggregate textures give real grip, especially where water gets tracked in during monsoon season.

  • A finished look

    A coated floor makes the whole space feel deliberate — something buyers notice when a home goes on the market.

Local flooring considerations

What the Kingman climate asks of a floor.

Heat and sun

Garage slabs regularly climb well past what an indoor floor ever sees, and standard epoxy will amber where UV hits it. That is why we usually finish garages with a UV-stable polyaspartic or aliphatic topcoat instead of leaving epoxy exposed.

Temperature swings

Winter nights can drop below freezing while afternoons warm up fast. Concrete moves with those cycles, and so do cracks. Flexible base coats and properly treated joints keep the coating from telegraphing every hairline.

Wind, dust and monsoon rain

Dust storms and late-summer downpours push grit and water into garages and onto patios. Textured finishes stay safer underfoot, and a sealed surface makes cleanup a hose-down instead of a scrubbing session.

Large lots and detached shops

Butler and Golden Valley properties often have detached garages, RV ports and metal shops. These slabs can be big, older or poured without a vapor barrier, so testing and the right primer matter even more.

Questions we hear often

Epoxy flooring FAQ

Straight answers about timing, durability and what to expect. Have something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

How long does a garage floor coating take in Kingman?

Most two- and three-car garages are a one- or two-day job. Polyaspartic and polyurea systems let us grind, coat, broadcast flake and topcoat in a single day, and you can walk on the floor the next morning. Plan on waiting a few more days before parking, longer if the forecast is cold.

Will the coating peel the way a store-bought kit did?

Kit failures almost always come down to prep. Acid etching and a quick rinse leave a smooth, weak surface, and hot tires eventually lift the film. We diamond-grind the slab to open it up, repair cracks and spalls, and use a primer built to bite into the concrete. That bond is what keeps a coating down.

Does the summer heat change how you install?

Yes. Resin cure speed roughly doubles as the slab warms, so in July and August we start early, keep material out of the sun and mix smaller batches. On very hot days we adjust which products we use rather than rushing the work. Winter brings the opposite problem, and some systems need the slab above a minimum temperature before they can go down.

Is an epoxy floor slippery?

A plain high-gloss coating can be slick when wet. Flake floors are naturally textured, and on any system we can add a fine aggregate to the topcoat. For patios, ramps and entries we usually recommend a heavier texture, since monsoon rain and tracked-in water are a fact of life here.

Can you coat a floor that already has paint or an old coating on it?

Usually, but the old material has to come off or be fully sound. We test adhesion, then grind away loose or failing film. Coating over a weak layer only means the new floor fails along with it.

Do you work outside Kingman city limits?

We regularly take on projects in Butler, Golden Valley, Valle Vista, the Hualapai Mountain area, Chloride, Dolan Springs and other nearby communities. If you are farther out, send us the details and we will tell you plainly whether the trip makes sense.

What does an estimate include?

We look at the slab in person, check for moisture and existing coatings, measure the space and talk through the systems that fit how you use it. You get a written price with the scope spelled out, including prep, repairs and the exact coating layers.
